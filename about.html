Hey, I'm Farukh

Hardware enthusiast and perpetual learner, curious about how code becomes real on hardware.

I've always wanted to know what actually happens after you hit "run" — how code turns into something real on a chip. That one question is basically why I'm here. I did my schooling and B.Tech in Delhi, then moved to Bangalore for my Master's because electronics just made sense to me in a way few other things did.

The honest version: I'm someone who can't stop learning. Circuits and computer architecture are the day job, but the curiosity doesn't switch off when I leave the lab — it just points at whatever happens to be interesting that week. I've come to think the subject matters less than the act of figuring something out.

Beyond the Lab

I play football when I get the chance and travel whenever I can. And when I want to think about something that isn't a timing violation, I read — lately a lot of political philosophy, mostly because I find arguments about how people ought to live together genuinely fun to chew on. Nothing fancy, just the usual ways of keeping the mind busy.

Current Chapter

College is behind me now. This July I'm joining Texas Instruments as a Digital Design Engineer — finally getting to watch RTL I write turn into silicon that actually ships. It's the part of the journey I've been waiting for since I first wondered what happens after you hit "run".

But this isn't the last stop. Industry is where I want to sharpen the instincts that textbooks can't teach. One day, though, I shall return — back to academia for a PhD, to chase the questions in computer architecture I haven't been able to stop thinking about. The goal stays the same as it's always been: learn everything there is to learn, build things that matter, and live a life I won't look back on with regret.
